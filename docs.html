How a coin fills its vault.

Every coin on Vaul is a normal Pons coin on Robinhood Chain with one twist: its creator fees stream into a vault that can only buy NFTs from the collection it was launched for. This page covers the whole path, from launch to the NFT landing in a holder's wallet.

Basics

One coin, one collection, one vault

Most memecoins pay their creator a cut of trading fees, and that is where the money stops. On Vaul the cut goes to work. You pick an NFT collection at launch, and a contract splits the coin's creator fees: 80% into a vault tied to that collection and 20% to the Vaul treasury. As the vault fills, it buys the cheapest listed NFT from the collection, one at a time.

  1. Launch

    Pick a collection, a policy and a creator tax. One transaction creates the coin on Pons plus its vault and fee router.

  2. Trade

    People buy and sell on the Pons bonding curve. Every trade pays a small fee.

  3. Harvest

    The fee router collects the creator share and splits it 80/20 between vault and treasury.

  4. Collect

    Once the vault can afford the floor, it buys the cheapest listing from its collection.

  5. Reward

    The NFT is raffled to holders, held forever or burned, whichever the creator locked in.

Trading your coin makes its vault buy floor NFTs. Holding your coin gives you a chance to win them.
Launch

Four steps, one signature

The Launch page walks you through it. Nothing is sent until you confirm the last step in your wallet.

01Your coin

Name, ticker and image. Upload straight from your phone; Pons stores the image with the coin. Add a description, links and a muse.ai video if you like.

02Collection

Choose the NFT collection the coin will collect. Search by name or filter by chain. Every listed collection trades on OpenSea.

03Rules

Pick what happens to the NFTs (raffle, hold or burn) and an optional creator tax of up to 10%.

04Confirm

Review and sign one transaction. You pay the Pons launch fee plus gas. Vaul charges nothing.

Locked forever. The collection, the NFT policy and the fee recipient cannot change after launch, not by the creator and not by Vaul.

Wallet capping gas at 1.2M? The launch page estimates the real amount and sends it with the transaction.

Money

Where every fee goes

Pons charges a fee on every buy and sell on the curve and passes the creator's share, plus any creator tax, to the coin's fee router. The router is created for your coin, set as its creator-fee recipient on Pons, and has no function that could hand that role to anyone else.

Vault 56%Vaul 14%Pons 30%

Share of the 1% trading fee with no creator tax: 0.0056 ETH to the vault, 0.0014 ETH to the treasury and 0.0030 ETH to Pons for every 1 ETH traded.

Harvest

harvest() sweeps the curve, claims from the Pons fee escrow and sends the split in one step. Anyone can call it, from the coin page or on-chain, and the keeper calls it about every 30 minutes.

Creator tax

An optional tax adds to the router's income in the same 80/20 split. Traders pay it, so a high tax can slow trading. Most coins use 0–2%.

Vault

Built to do exactly one thing

Each coin has its own vault. The only thing it can do with ETH is buy NFTs from its collection, and the contract checks these rules on every purchase:

  • Pays only through an allow-listed marketplace: Seaport 1.6, the protocol behind OpenSea.
  • Never pays above a price ceiling the keeper posts from live floors. A ceiling expires after 1 hour.
  • Reverts unless it ends up owning the exact NFT from its own collection, for no more than the stated price.
  • Rejects NFTs from any other collection.
  • Has no withdraw function. ETH leaves only as payment for NFTs.

What happens to the NFTs

Raffle

Each NFT is given to a holder, drawn on-chain with tickets by balance. The most popular choice.

Hold

NFTs stay in the vault for good, a permanent on-chain collection owned by the coin itself.

Burn

Every NFT bought is destroyed, shrinking the collection's supply like a buyback-and-burn.

Every balance, harvest, purchase and giveaway shows on the coin page and in Activity, read straight from the chain.

Giveaways

Nobody picks the winner

Not the creator, not the keeper, not Vaul. A raffle runs in five public steps:

  1. Snapshot

    The keeper replays every transfer of the coin and gives each wallet tickets by balance. Contracts such as the curve, pools and the vault get none. The Merkle root goes on-chain; the full list with proofs is published for anyone to check.

  2. 15 minutes public

    The snapshot must be public for 15 minutes before a draw can start.

  3. Commit

    Anyone calls commitDraw, pinning a Robinhood Chain block 20 blocks ahead. Nobody can know its hash yet.

  4. Draw

    Anyone calls draw; the winning ticket comes from that block's hash. If nobody draws within 256 blocks, the commit resets and is tried again.

  5. Delivery

    The keeper sends the NFT using the winner's proof. Winners can also claim it on Giveaways.

Won an NFT on Solana? Open Giveaways with the winning wallet and enter your Solana address once. The keeper sends it there.
Other chains

Collecting beyond Robinhood Chain

Coins always live on Robinhood Chain but can collect on Ethereum, Base, Hyperliquid and Solana. A Robinhood Chain contract cannot buy on another chain directly, so these coins use a vault with extra safeguards:

  1. Announce

    When the vault can afford the floor, the keeper announces a withdrawal of the exact amount. It shows on the coin page.

  2. Wait 1 hour

    The money cannot move for an hour, and the registry owner can cancel it if anything looks wrong.

  3. Bridge and buy

    The keeper bridges with Relay and buys the cheapest OpenSea listing on the target chain.

  4. Record

    The purchase is written back to the vault with its transaction hash, so every buy has a public receipt.

More trust than Robinhood collections. While funds are bridged and the NFT waits on the other chain, they sit in the keeper's wallet there. The delay, the cancel option and the receipts limit this, but it is not as trustless as a Robinhood Chain vault.
Keeper

The bot that keeps vaults moving

An automated job runs every 30 minutes and does the routine work. Every job it does can also be done by anyone else.

Does

  • Harvests fees for every coin
  • Posts a 1-hour price ceiling from live floors
  • Buys the cheapest listing once a vault can afford it
  • Runs snapshots, commits, draws and deliveries
  • Bridges, buys and records receipts on other chains

Cannot

  • Withdraw ETH from a Robinhood Chain vault
  • Change a coin's collection or policy
  • Send an NFT anywhere except to a raffle winner with a valid proof

Where the numbers come from

  • Charts are live GeckoTerminal embeds, first on the Pons curve, then on the pool after graduation.
  • Floors on the home page come from OpenSea and refresh every 6 hours; USD values use current rates.
  • Activity, Gallery and Analytics are built from on-chain events, so they always match the chain.
  • Collections are curated for activity; the registry owner can add more.

Risks

Read before you ape

  • The contracts are open source and tested, including on a fork of mainnet, but not independently audited.
  • The keeper picks the price ceiling and the listing. The contract bounds this, but a dishonest keeper could overpay up to the ceiling.
  • For other chains, funds and NFTs sit in the keeper's wallet while bridging and until delivery.
  • After graduation, pool fees reach the escrow when Pons's operator sweeps them.
  • Memecoins are extremely volatile and can go to zero. Nothing here is financial advice.
FAQ

Quick answers

Can the creator take money out of the vault?

No. A Robinhood Chain vault has no withdraw function. Its ETH can only leave as payment for NFTs from its own collection.

How much does it cost to launch?

Only the Pons launch fee (currently 0.0005 ETH) plus network gas. Vaul charges nothing to launch.

Do I earn anything as a creator?

Not directly. The creator share goes to the vault and treasury. A coin that collects blue-chip NFTs and gives them to holders tends to attract more trading and more holders.

How do I get a chance to win NFTs?

Hold a coin whose policy is Raffle. Each time its vault buys an NFT, a snapshot is taken and your tickets follow your balance at that moment.

When does the vault buy?

As soon as its balance covers the cheapest listing under the posted ceiling. The coin page shows the balance and the progress toward the next buy.

Can a collection be removed?

The registry owner can stop listing a collection for new launches. Existing coins keep collecting the collection they launched with.

Can I add a video to my coin?

Yes. Upload it to muse.ai and paste the link (muse.ai/v/…) at launch, or later on your coin page. Only the wallet that launched the coin can change it, by signing a message (no gas). The video is kept on Vaul, not on-chain.

What if the keeper stops?

Harvest, commit, draw and claim are public functions. Anyone can call them, so fees and prizes are never stuck.

Glossary

Floor
The lowest listed price for an NFT in a collection.
Bonding curve
Pons's automatic market for a new coin: price rises as people buy and falls as they sell.
Graduation
When the curve fills up and liquidity moves to a regular pool.
Fee router
The contract that receives a coin's creator fees and splits them 80/20.
Vault
The contract that holds a coin's ETH and the NFTs it buys.
Keeper
The bot that performs routine calls such as harvests, buys and draws.
Merkle root
A short fingerprint of the ticket list that lets anyone prove their tickets.
Seaport
The open marketplace protocol behind OpenSea, used by vaults to buy.
