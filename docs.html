Everything about Vaul

Vaul turns a memecoin into a collector. Every coin launched here is a normal Pons coin on Robinhood Chain, with one difference: its creator fees flow into a vault that can only ever buy NFTs from the one collection it was launched for, and then hands them to the people who hold the coin.

01 Overview

Most memecoins pay their creator a cut of trading fees and that is where the money ends. Vaul routes that cut somewhere more interesting. When you launch, you pick an NFT collection. The coin's creator fees are collected by a contract that splits them: 80% into a vault tied to your collection and 20% to the Vaul treasury. As the vault fills up, it buys floor NFTs from that collection, one at a time, at the cheapest listed price.

What happens to those NFTs is decided at launch and locked forever. They can be raffled to the coin's holders, kept in the vault as a growing on-chain collection, or burned to shrink the collection's supply.

In one sentence

Trading your coin makes its vault buy floor NFTs, and holding your coin gives you a chance to win them.

02 How it works

  1. 1Launch

    Pick a collection, a policy and a creator tax. One transaction creates the coin on Pons plus its vault and fee router.

  2. 2Trade

    People buy and sell the coin on the Pons bonding curve. Every trade pays a small fee.

  3. 3Harvest

    The fee router collects the creator share and splits it: 80% to the vault, 20% to the treasury.

  4. 4Collect

    Once the vault can afford the floor, it buys the cheapest listed NFT from its collection.

  5. 5Reward

    The NFT is raffled to holders, held forever, or burned, whichever the creator locked in.

03 Launching a coin

The Launch page walks you through four short steps. Nothing is sent until you confirm the final transaction in your wallet.

Step 1Your coin

Name, ticker and image. You can upload a picture straight from your phone; it is resized and stored for you, and Pons saves it with the coin. Add a description, links and a muse.ai video if you like.

Step 2Collection

Choose which NFT collection the coin will collect. Search by name or filter by chain. Every listed collection is active and tradable on OpenSea.

Step 3Rules

Choose what happens to the NFTs (raffle, hold or burn) and an optional creator tax of up to 10% on top of the normal trading fee.

Step 4Confirm

Review everything, then sign one transaction. You pay only the Pons launch fee plus gas. Vaul charges nothing to launch.

Locked forever

The collection, the NFT policy and the fee recipient cannot be changed after launch, not even by the creator or by Vaul. Double-check them before you confirm.

Using a wallet that caps gas (some mobile wallets stop at 1.2M gas)? The launch page estimates the real amount and sends it with the transaction, so the launch fits.

04 Fees and the fee router

Pons charges a trading fee on every buy and sell on the curve and passes the creator's share of it, plus any creator tax, to the coin's fee router. The router is a small contract created for your coin. It is set as the coin's creator-fee recipient on Pons and has no function that could ever hand that role to someone else.

harvest() moves the money. It sweeps the curve, claims from the Pons fee escrow and sends the split in one step. Anyone can call it, from the Harvest button on any coin page or directly on-chain. The keeper also calls it automatically about every 30 minutes, so you never have to.

A creator tax adds to the router's income in the same 80/20 split. It is charged to traders, so a high tax can slow trading. Most coins use 0–2%.

05 The vault

Each coin has its own vault. It is deliberately boring: the only thing it can do with ETH is buy NFTs from its collection, under strict rules that the contract checks on every purchase.

  • It can only pay through an allow-listed marketplace, Seaport 1.6 (the protocol behind OpenSea).
  • Every purchase must be at or below a price ceiling that the keeper posts from live floor data. A ceiling expires after 1 hour.
  • The purchase reverts unless the vault ends up owning the exact NFT from its own collection and spent no more than the stated price.
  • It rejects NFTs from any other collection.
  • There is no withdraw function. ETH leaves only as payment for NFTs.

You can watch every vault live: its balance, each harvest, each purchase and each giveaway appear on the coin page and in Activity, read straight from the chain.

06 NFT policies

🎁Raffle to holders

Each NFT the vault buys is given away. Holders get tickets in proportion to their balance, and a winner is drawn on-chain. The most popular choice: it rewards the community directly.

🏛Hold forever

The NFTs stay in the vault for good, building a permanent on-chain collection owned by nobody but the coin itself.

🔥Burn

Every NFT bought is destroyed, permanently reducing the collection's supply, like a buyback-and-burn for the collection.

07 Giveaways

Raffles are built so that nobody, not the creator, not the keeper and not Vaul, can choose the winner.

  1. Snapshot

    When the vault gets an NFT, the keeper replays every transfer of the coin and gives each wallet tickets in proportion to its balance. Contracts such as the curve, pools and the vault get none. The snapshot's Merkle root goes on-chain and the full list, with proofs, is published as a file anyone can check.

  2. Public window, 15 minutes

    The snapshot must be public for 15 minutes before a draw can start, so anyone can verify it.

  3. Commit

    Anyone calls commitDraw, which pins a Robinhood Chain block 20 blocks in the future. Nobody can know that block's hash yet.

  4. Draw

    Once the block exists, anyone calls draw. The winning ticket is derived from that block's hash. If nobody draws within 256 blocks, the commit simply resets and is tried again.

  5. Delivery

    The keeper delivers the NFT to the winner using the winner's Merkle proof. Winners can also claim it themselves on the Giveaways page.

Won an NFT on Solana?

Solana uses different addresses, so open the Giveaways page with the winning wallet and enter your Solana address once. The keeper then sends the NFT there.

08 Collections on other chains

Coins always live on Robinhood Chain, but they can collect NFTs on Ethereum, Base, Hyperliquid and Solana too. Because a Robinhood Chain contract cannot buy on another chain directly, these coins use a special vault with extra safeguards:

  1. Announce

    When the vault can afford the floor, the keeper announces a withdrawal of the exact amount needed. It is shown on the coin page.

  2. Wait 1 hour

    The money cannot move for 1 hour. During that time the registry owner can cancel it if anything looks wrong.

  3. Bridge and buy

    The keeper bridges the funds with Relay and buys the cheapest listing on OpenSea on the target chain.

  4. Record

    The purchase is written back to the vault on Robinhood Chain with its transaction hash, so every buy has a public receipt.

More trust than Robinhood collections

While funds are bridged and the NFT waits on the other chain, they are held by the keeper's wallet there. The delay, the cancel option and the public receipts limit this, but it is not as trustless as a Robinhood Chain vault.

09 The keeper

The keeper is an automated bot that runs every 30 minutes. It does the routine work so coins keep collecting without anyone lifting a finger.

JobWhat it does
HarvestCollects fees for every coin and splits them into vault and treasury.
Price ceilingReads live floors and posts a 1-hour ceiling for each vault.
SweepBuys the cheapest listing once a vault can afford it.
GiveawaysTakes snapshots, opens raffles, commits and draws, and delivers prizes.
Other chainsAnnounces withdrawals, bridges, buys and records receipts.

What it cannot do: withdraw ETH from a Robinhood Chain vault, change any coin's collection or policy, or send an NFT anywhere except to a raffle winner with a valid proof. Every job it does can also be done by anyone else.

10 Prices and data

  • Charts: each coin page embeds a live GeckoTerminal chart for the coin, first on its Pons curve and later on its pool after graduation.
  • Top floors: floor prices, owners and volume on the home page come from OpenSea and refresh every 6 hours. USD values use current exchange rates.
  • Activity, Gallery and Analytics: built entirely from on-chain events, so they always match the chain.
  • Collections: the list of collections is curated for activity; a new collection can be added by the registry owner.

11 Contracts

All contracts are deployed on Robinhood Chain mainnet (chain id 4663). Tap an address to open it in the explorer.

12 Security and risks

  • The contracts are open source and tested, including against a fork of Robinhood Chain mainnet, but they have not been independently audited.
  • The keeper chooses the price ceiling and which listing to buy. The contract bounds this, but a dishonest keeper could overpay up to the ceiling.
  • For collections on other chains, funds and NFTs are held by the keeper's wallet during bridging and until delivery (see Other chains).
  • After a coin graduates to its pool, pool fees reach the escrow when Pons's operator sweeps them.
  • Memecoins are extremely volatile and can go to zero. Nothing here is financial advice.

13 FAQ

Can the creator take money out of the vault?

No. A Robinhood Chain vault has no withdraw function. Its ETH can only leave as payment for NFTs from its own collection.

How much does it cost to launch?

Only the Pons launch fee (currently 0.0005 ETH) plus network gas. Vaul charges nothing to launch.

Do I earn anything as a creator?

Not directly. The creator share of fees goes to the vault and treasury. The idea is that a coin that collects blue-chip NFTs and gives them to holders attracts more trading and more holders.

How do I get a chance to win NFTs?

Hold a coin whose policy is Raffle. Every time its vault buys an NFT, a snapshot is taken and your tickets are based on your balance at that moment.

When does the vault buy?

As soon as its balance covers the cheapest listing under the posted ceiling. The coin page shows the vault balance and the progress toward the next buy.

Can a collection be removed?

The registry owner can stop listing a collection for new launches. Existing coins keep collecting the collection they were launched with.

Can I add a video to my coin?

Yes. Upload it to muse.ai and paste its link (muse.ai/v/…) when you launch, or later with "Add a muse.ai video" on your coin page. It plays at the top of the coin page. Only the wallet that launched the coin can add, change or remove the video; you sign a message, which costs no gas. The video is kept on Vaul, not on-chain, so it does not show on Pons.

What if the keeper stops?

Harvest, commit, draw and claim are all public functions. Anyone can call them, so fees and prizes are never stuck.

14 Glossary

Floor
The lowest price at which an NFT from a collection is currently listed for sale.
Bonding curve
Pons's automatic market for a new coin: the price rises as people buy and falls as they sell.
Graduation
When a coin's curve fills up, its liquidity moves to a regular trading pool.
Fee router
The contract that receives a coin's creator fees and splits them 80/20.
Vault
The contract that holds a coin's ETH and the NFTs it buys.
Keeper
The automated bot that performs routine calls such as harvests, buys and draws.
Merkle root
A short fingerprint of the full ticket list. It lets anyone prove their tickets without storing the whole list on-chain.
Seaport
The open marketplace protocol used by OpenSea, through which vaults buy NFTs.
